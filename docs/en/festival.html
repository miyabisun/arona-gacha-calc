5.5th Anniversary Festival Comparison

miyabisun/arona-gacha-calc

Festival recruitment doubles the 3★ rate to 6%, and festival students you did not select can also appear. Because such an off-target pull can still hand you another student you wanted, the 200-pull milestones of Recruitment Points become competitive.

Assumptions
  • The 3★ rate during festival recruitment is 6.00%.
  • The student you select appears at 0.70%. Under Recruitment Charge, that becomes 50% at charge 99 and 100% at charge 199.
  • The 9 remaining festival students — 10 in total, minus the one you selected — share 0.90% equally, giving 0.10% each.
  • The remaining 4.40% covers permanent 3★ students. It is recorded for completeness but never used in the calculation.
  • The first-time pickup bonus is granted only by pulling the student you selected or exchanging Recruitment Points. An off-target pull never grants it.
  • The strategy always selects a student you do not own yet; once every student is owned, it selects an owned one to collect the remaining bonuses.
  • Eleph and shards are counted separately. Most players sit on a large shard stock, so shards cannot carry the same weight as Eleph.

Recruitment Charge gives you no exit

Recruitment Charge has no exchange, so every student you want has to be selected and pulled in turn. Start from the expected cost of simply owning them, then see what changes when an off-target pull lands.

The case for Recruitment Charge: it costs less

Everything above assumed the same number of pulls. The case for the new system is that fewer pulls are needed in the first place — and that is true. If you can walk away once every student is owned, the Pyroxene you never spent carries straight into the next limited or standard banner.

Recruitment Points lets you choose your upside

So the natural question is whether the old system has upside of its own. It does: Recruitment Points lets you stay on Swimsuit Iroha the whole way while directing each 200-point exchange where it helps most. Recruitment Charge offers no such choice.

That said, letting a festival student slip away is never an option. If Ibuki is not owned, the first exchange always goes to securing her. The new system reaches that same point reliably too, so nothing separates them here.

The difference shows up in the second exchange. What it is worth depends on who you spend it on: a student you do not own yields the unit plus a 100 Eleph first-time bonus, while one you already own whose bonus is still unspent — say a player sitting on Uniform Nel at UE3 — pays 100 Eleph for the duplicate plus the 100 Eleph bonus, for 200 Eleph.

Where the run actually ends

Everything so far assumed a full 400 pulls. In practice you stop at the end of the block where the last student arrives. Nobody spends another 200 pulls on a finished roster just to farm Eleph. Each exchange still goes to a student you do not own, whenever one remains.

Either way the haul lands around 300 Eleph. If simply owning Ibuki is enough, the job is done. If Ibuki also needs UE2, that falls well short of 340 Eleph and this banner alone will not finish the job.

At three students the approaches split apart. Moving on after each hit raises the chance of stopping at 200 pulls to 50.6% and costs 36,640 Pyroxene on average. Staying on Iroha halves that release rate to 25.7% and pushes the cost to 44,477 Pyroxene — but carries away 76 more Eleph.

Put another way, it buys 76 Eleph for 7,837 Pyroxene. That is a fair trade if an attacker needs UE2 soon, but if the goal is to own everyone and carry Pyroxene into the next banner, switching as each student lands is cheaper. Having that call to make at all belongs to Recruitment Points alone.
